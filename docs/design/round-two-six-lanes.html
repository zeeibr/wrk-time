Design direction · six lanes · pick one

Six convictions about what this app is for.

One planner, one interval timer, one fasting clock, one weight log — built around two 2 lb dumbbells, a 15 lb beam, three rings, and a walking pad. What separates these lanes isn't decoration. Each puts a different thing at the center and lets everything else arrange itself behind it.

Four screens per lane at true iPhone size, including the lock screen, because a workout timer lives there as much as it lives in the app. Sample data throughout.

A

Interval — the instrument

The timer is the product; everything else is preparation for it. Built to be read from the floor, at arm's length, mid-set, with your hands full. A machined dial with an engraved bezel, ivory numerals cut in mono, and a sodium-amber signal that reads instantly as work — rest cools to teal, so you learn the state from color temperature before you read the word. Off the clock it goes dark and quiet so nothing competes with the count.

Palette

#131110 warm ink#1D1A17 case#F3EDE3 ivory#F2A03D work#6FB3A8 rest

Warm ink, never neutral black. Secondary text sits at 9.3:1 and tertiary at 5.7:1 against the case — legibility was measured, not eyeballed.

Type

0:41Beam front squat

Mono numerals so digits never shift width mid-count. Condensed caps at 0.13–0.34em tracking for labels — dense without shouting. Scale runs 11 px to 84 px.

The dial

Six stacked layers: masked conic progress, a machined repeating-conic face, a radial specular sheen, sixty engraved tick lines in SVG, and a filtered bloom on the sweep marker. In Swift it ships as a single Canvas draw rather than composed views.

Motion & cost

Exactly one thing moves — a four-second breath on the live WORK dot, off under reduced motion. The dial itself is deliberately still. Build is 2–3× a conventional ring timer; the rest of the app is standard.

Gives up warmth. The instrument metaphor is gorgeous at the timer and slightly overbearing elsewhere — a beginner meets a machine before they meet a coach.

B

Meridian — the circadian one

The app knows what time it is in your body, and shows it. The ground is the sky right now — indigo before dawn, washed blue at noon, deep amber as the eating window closes — so the interface is never neutral about timing. One 24-hour ring carries fast, feed, train and light on the same circle, which is the functional-medicine argument made visual instead of written. The lane a reviewer could describe in a single sentence.

Palette

#0D1730 night#3E4C77 fasted#8FB8D6 daylight#FFB25C solar#FBF6EF paper

Not a light and dark mode — one continuous ground derived from local sunrise and sunset, with the ink system re-solved per band so contrast never dips.

Type

14:22 fastedWindow opens 12:30

Optima for display — humanist, warm, a little clinical; reads as care rather than performance. One Didot moment on the dusk screen, earned because reflection is that screen's job.

The 24-hour ring

Four concentric tracks — fast, feed, train, sleep — plus a light band, drawn as real arithmetic: per-segment stroke-dasharray derived from wall-clock hours, with a now-marker bloom. The sky behind it is four blended layers plus feTurbulence grain.

Motion & cost

One 6.5-second breath on the sun bloom and the timer endpoint, gated behind reduced-motion. The ground shifts across the day, never inside a session. Build is 2–3× a flat lane, though sky and ring both collapse to one parameterized component in production.

Gives up simplicity. Because the ground is never neutral this is two design systems wearing one skin — the daylight screen needed a wholly inverted ink system, and every new component costs double.

C

Baseline — the protocol

Your body as a document you can actually read. Swiss, printed, unhurried — a strict grid, one grotesque at four weights, hairline rules, and color only where it carries meaning: on target, drifting, back off. It treats you as someone running a protocol on themselves rather than someone who needs cheering on, which is the honest read of a person who owns a smart scale and a Whoop band. The quietest lane, and the one that ages best.

Palette

#FAFAF8 paper#DDE2E0 rule#0E1413 ink#1B3A8C active#2E6F53 on target#99560F caution

Cool-biased white, never cream. Caution appears exactly once in the whole app — on "back off" for stress load.

Type

0:41Beam front squat · 15 lb

One grotesque at four weights — 200 for the 108 px timer, 500 for titles. Mono is for labels, units and axis text only, never body copy.

Editorial devices

A running marginal index down the left hairline, carried into the signals table as a section column. A FIG. 01 rule-and-caption frame around the weight plate. Hanging figures with units in a separate column. The lock screen inverts to a paper card on ink, so the Live Activity reads as the same document rather than a notification.

Motion & cost

No motion at all — a protocol you read should hold still. Build is moderate-to-high: no libraries, but the weight plate is hand-plotted and the ruled tables demand real optical-alignment discipline. Roughly 1.5× a conventional card build.

Gives up swagger. Authoritative to someone who already owns a Whoop; cold to someone who wanted encouragement.

D

Signal — the kinetic poster

Training is an act of will, and the app is a starting pistol. It says one thing at a time, enormously, then gets out of the way. Every screen is composed like printed matter — flat fields of ink, a hard grid, type set at sizes that would be absurd anywhere else. The interval doesn't tick down in a widget; it drains the whole screen. The loudest lane, and the only one built to be screenshotted.

Palette

#1A38C8 cobalt#F0EDE4 bone#0D0D0F black#FF5A3C coral · live only

Four inks, flat fields, no gradient or shadow anywhere. Coral is spent only on a running interval, which is why the Body screen has no warm accent at all.

Type

0:41Remaining of 60 s work

Two faces only: condensed display from 60 to 140 px at −0.035 to −0.055em, and mono for every label with an 11 px floor. No third face, no exceptions.

The knockout

The interval is a coral field anchored to the bottom edge at exactly 68.33% — 41 of 60 seconds — and the 140 px numeral is set so the field's hard edge cuts straight through the digits: coral on black above the line, black on coral below. Two stacked layers, the lower one clipped to the field, so the type stays one continuous form. In SwiftUI it's a single mask on a duplicated text layer.

Motion & cost

One blinking dot on the Live Activity, stepped over 1.6 s and floored at 42% opacity so it never reads as missing. Nothing else moves. Cheapest lane to build — flat fields, hairlines and system type, no blur, no imagery.

Gives up gentleness. It has nerve, but a poster shouts the same volume on the morning you don't want to be shouted at.

E

Season — the living system

Consistency beats intensity, so the app rewards showing up rather than performing. The program is a living thing you tend across a season — progress is a generative form that gains a mark for every completed session and visibly becomes something over eighty-four days. The scale is present but deliberately not the hero. The only lane designed around the real risk for someone twelve days in: quitting.

Palette

#0F1A15 forest ink#35513F moss#8FA894 sage#E9E5D9 oat#D9A227 saffron

Oat rather than cream, saffron rather than terracotta, and sage is never used for body text — only ghost rings and hairline icons.

Type

Eleven days, unbroken.Season one · day 12 of 84

A slab, not a delicate old-style serif — it reads sturdy and warm rather than precious. Sans for UI, mono for small data only.

The growth form

Twelve nesting, slightly eccentric rings drawn as Catmull-Rom curves over a shared distortion field. Each week opens at a golden-angle rotation and stays open until its sixth session closes it. One mark is one finished session — a radial notch crossing its own week's ring. Weeks you haven't lived yet sit as faint hairlines, so you can see the shape you're growing into.

Motion & cost

The mark is laid down live as the round completes; nothing else moves. Moderate build — ordinary SwiftUI shell, but the form needs a real generative drawing layer at three render scales, which is a week of its own rather than a chart-library call.

Gives up reassurance. The organism is honest at day 12, so the hero screen is mostly promise — someone who wants a number to feel good about has to take the ghost rings on faith.

F

Coach — the companion

The other five lanes are dashboards with an AI bolted on. This one inverts it: the conversation is the app. You say "shoulder's sore, swap tomorrow" and the program changes. The timer, the fasting clock and the weight chart are rich components that live inside the thread rather than destinations you navigate to — and the coach's words are set in a reading serif while everything the machine says stays in sans, so you can hear the difference between a person and a system.

Palette

#F3F4F2 paper#1A1D1C ink#14342B pine#E4E8E5 stone#C98A2B amber · live

Pine is the user's own voice and every primary control. Stone is reserved for machine surfaces, so you can tell at a glance which parts of the page the coach wrote and which the system generated.

Two voices, two faces

Best sleep of the block — 7:12, and an HRV of 64.Tue · day 12 of 84 · 22 min

The coach speaks in a reading serif with no bubble at all — just a 3 px pine rule in the margin. Everything the machine says stays in sans and mono. You hear the difference before you read it.

Inline components

Four in one family, all stone with a mono eyebrow and an authored glyph: session card with numbered blocks and equipment in the mono column; fasting clock with a 24-hour band and an amber now-marker; plan revision showing was struck through against now; weight chart with gridlines, area fill and a labelled endpoint. The full-screen timer keeps the coach's serif cue line at the top, so leaving the thread never leaves the design.

Motion & cost

Components stay live inside a scrolling transcript rather than animating for effect. Highest build of the six — 1.5–2× a tabbed dashboard, plus a versioned program model behind the revision diff, plus real token cost on every exchange.

Gives up addressability. If a thing hasn't been discussed recently it has no location — there's no way to answer "what did week one look like?" without asking and waiting.

Once you pick

Approving a lane locks the visual system; everything after is engineering against it. Hybrids are on the table and the lanes were drawn to graft cleanly — they share a screen anatomy and a tab structure. If I get one vote: E as the spine, A's mono numerals in the timer, and F's coach as a tab rather than the whole architecture.

  • FirstDesign system in code

    Color, type scale, spacing and components as Swift tokens, so the lane is enforced rather than remembered.

  • ThenTimer engine

    Drift-free interval clock, background audio, haptics, Live Activity and Dynamic Island — the part that must never be wrong.

  • ThenData and Health

    SwiftData models with CloudKit sync, HealthKit reads for weight, sleep, HRV and heart rate from the scale and Whoop.

  • ThenClaude planner

    Equipment-aware program generation with a strict schema, weekly re-planning from your logged trend, and a deterministic offline fallback.

  • LastWatch app

    Standalone interval runner with workout session, heart rate and haptic cues, syncing completion back to the phone.

Sample figures throughout. Fasting and nutrition content is framed as education, never medical advice — that framing is both the right call and what keeps a health app through review. iOS can't be compiled in this environment, so the first build happens on your Mac.
